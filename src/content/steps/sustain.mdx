Park in a Truck parks strive to be an urban oasis filled with plants and trees that nourish everyone who visits them —
including the birds, bees, butterflies, bats and other pollinators the plants depend on. Like a baby, your park needs
lots of love and attention in its first few years of life in order to survive, thrive, and become social!

This step is broken into three categories, each best coordinated by one person:

- **Survive** — the only *critical* category. Weekly watering and weeding for the first two years. If no one in the
  community can take this on, you'll need to hire someone for the park's infancy.
- **Thrive** — optional extra effort: annuals, bulbs, and starting an edible garden from seed.
- **Socialize** — entirely up to you: the events and meetings that turn your park into a gathering place.

## Water & weed every week

**Watering.** Water your plants weekly for the first two years. Pay attention to see if they need more or less:

- If the top 2 inches of soil are dry, it's time to water.
- If plants are visibly wilting, it's time to water.
- Use a rainwater guide to track how much water your plants are getting.

If you need to water, thoroughly soak the planting area — either run water for 3 hours using a soaker hose, or water
by hand, slowly enough that it doesn't run off, long enough to penetrate 6–8 inches into the soil.

**Weeding.** Weeding must be done weekly from April 15th to October, for at least the first two years.

- Use a pre-emergent on your beds to control weeds (see seasonal cleanup, below).
- Instead of pulling weeds, try spraying them with a solution of 20% vinegar. If you're having trouble, consult your
  local nursery.
- Consult online weed resources or an app like PlantSnap to identify plants and weeds.

<Callout kind="tip">
  [Pre-emergent for flower beds ↗](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B003S3U02E/) ·
  [Pre-emergent for veggie gardens ↗](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B083PKMJTM/) ·
  [20% vinegar ↗](https://www.amazon.com/Green-Gobbler-Vinegar-Glyphosate-Herbicide/dp/B09HDFQ531/) ·
  [Common weeds (USDA) ↗](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/Internet/FSE_DOCUMENTS/nrcs142p2_017662.pdf) *(link currently dead)*
</Callout>

### Mulching

Mulch all planting beds with leaf mold or mature, triple-shredded hardwood mulch, and maintain it at a depth of 2 to
3 inches — being careful not to over-mulch. Be especially careful mulching around trees: the wrong way is to push
mulch up onto the trunk like a mountain slope; the correct way is a flat donut, with an inch or more of open space
between the mulch and the trunk. Mulch left touching the trunk can rot the bark and expose the tree to insect damage,
disease and possible death.

<Callout kind="tip">[How to lay mulch ↗](https://skh.com/thedirt/how-to-lay-mulch/)</Callout>

### Pruning

Use hand-pruning tools for all shrub pruning — don't use power trimmers. Prune shrubs and trees only as necessary to
guide future structural shape, or to remove dead or diseased material. Prune and trim branches to maintain a minimum
head clearance of 7 feet along walks and 13 feet over roads and driveways.

<Callout kind="tip">[How to prune like a pro ↗](https://youtube.com/watch?v=BE2K_mCIppM)</Callout>

### Pest control

The park is designed to attract pollinators who help with pest control. Sometimes human intervention is still
needed — if you see something strange on your plants, investigate: take photos, research online, and/or contact your
local nursery.

<Callout kind="tip">[Pests and disease guide ↗](https://extension.psu.edu/trees-lawns-and-landscaping/home-gardening/pests-and-diseases)</Callout>

## Grow more: seeds, bulbs & annuals

If you're willing to put in a little extra effort, here's how to make your park really thrive. Annuals give you
showy flowers that bloom all season but don't return the next year. Bulbs, like tulips and daffodils, typically come
back every year and add early-season color. Starting a vegetable garden from seed indoors is a cost-effective way to
grow exactly what you want.

**Seeds.** Read seed catalogs to decide what to grow and how to care for it. Order seeds for veggies and annuals.
Start your seeds indoors for an edible garden, then transfer to planters once the weather warms.

**Bulbs.** Order bulbs and store them in a cool, dry space. Plant bulbs between Halloween and Thanksgiving. Fertilize
4–6 weeks before the anticipated bloom. Deadhead flowers after bloom (remove the spent flower heads). Wait for the
foliage to turn yellow, then remove the entire plant.

**Annuals.** Buy your desired annual flowers. Plant in April if spring is warm, or June if it's cool. Keep well
watered, and monitor for pests — remove infested foliage if needed.

## Park Ambassador program

We highly recommend the Park Ambassador program, which hires local youth to help maintain and program the park.
Park Ambassadors:

- Create a safe, clean and accessible park for everyone.
- Plan fun activities for community residents of all ages.
- Keep the park clean and neat.
- Make sure the park is a welcoming green oasis.

We've developed a Park Ambassador Guidebook — a step-by-step curriculum and resource guide to help Park Ambassadors
maintain and program the new park, and a place to record what happens there. The ambassador youth work closely with a
community member, who acts as their mentor and guide through the whole process.
