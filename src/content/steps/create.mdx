Building a park is a great way to connect neighbors, friends and others. The process requires dedicated volunteers
who can give their time and energy, with a wide variety of skills — carpentry, landscaping, organizing, encouraging,
leading. Like following a recipe, park building is a step-by-step sequence; most Park in a Truck parks go up in about
6–8 weekends.

The Create chapter is all about building your park. We broke the process down into eight phases. Each phase can be
completed in roughly a weekend — the size of your park and number of volunteers will affect how long it actually
takes. The phases are ordered for a reason; review each one and adjust your schedule as needed.

This book assumes you've done the cost estimate and know the quantities and materials you'll need to order for each
phase. If you haven't, go back to the Dream step and use the cost estimator.

## Volunteer release form

Make sure you have volunteer release forms available at the site whenever you're working on the park. This form
releases you from liability for injuries to your volunteers. It's your responsibility to consult your organization's
legal advisors and insurance carrier before starting construction — they may want you to take additional steps to
protect the organization from liability. Give your volunteers clear instructions about expectations and tasks for the
work day; they're more likely to come back in future weeks when they feel their time was put to good use.

## Phase 1: Organize

As in any process, the first and most important step is to organize your team by identifying project leaders and
establishing a schedule. How many people you assign to these roles depends on the complexity and size of your
project.

- **Construction manager.** Orders materials, is there for deliveries, gives instructions to volunteers on build
  days, and coordinates with the project manager on schedule.
- **Project manager.** In charge of getting volunteers, social media, ordering food, handing out waivers, coordinates
  finances and controls the schedule.

## Phase 2: Prepare the lot

### Steps

**Step 1: Evaluate.** Some sites need more clearing or grading than others, which can require special machinery —
contact a landscape contractor if any of the following can't be done by the community alone.

- Contact the PiaT team ([ParkinaTruck@jefferson.edu](mailto:ParkinaTruck@jefferson.edu)) for a site visit
  consultation if you're unsure how to prepare your site.
- Contact Pennsylvania One Call at **811** to mark utility locations.

<Field id="create.one-call-address" label="Address for your PA One Call request" auto="lot.address" />

**Step 2: Protect.** Bring small tools and equipment to the site. Set up protective construction fencing with wood
posts at tree trunks, buildings and other items that need protection. Install silt/erosion control fencing around
the site and at all drain inlets.

**Step 3: Removal.** Remove unwanted vegetation (including roots), tree stumps, trash and masonry materials — some
vegetation removal may require herbicide treatment to control effectively in a timely manner. Prune existing trees to
remove limbs from the trunk to at least 6' high. Haul debris and vegetation off site to the proper dumping location;
stockpile masonry materials on site to use as gabion basket fill.

**Step 4: Site leveling.** Begin grade raking and leveling the site. Make sure any Pennsylvania One Call markings are
still intact and visible — soil grade should be relatively level with the sidewalk grade.

## Phase 3: Layout & gravel base

### Steps

**Step 1: Materials.** Bring equipment and tools to the site; prepare for deliveries. Locate a stockpile area for
gravel and cover it with a tarp if possible. Mark the general areas to receive gravel in spray paint — if there's not
enough room, coordinate the delivery with your city to have it placed in the street.

<Callout kind="note" title="Blocking the street for a delivery?">
  Apply for a temporary no-parking permit so the delivery truck has somewhere to unload.
  [Apply on phila.gov ↗](https://www.phila.gov/services/cars-parking-transportation/apply-for-a-parking-permit/apply-for-a-temporary-no-parking-permit/)
</Callout>
<Field id="create.delivery-address" label="Address for the no-parking permit" auto="lot.address" />

**Step 2: Layout planting beds & park element locations.** Begin at the starting point you marked in the Assess
workbook. Refer to your plan for the locations and lengths of planting-bed and park-element edges. Use stakes and
string line to mark the locations of edging and park elements you'll add in later phases.

**Step 3: Install sub base.** Prepare ground surfaces for gabion baskets per the assembly instructions. Dig out
entrances to allow for the change in level from the new park to the sidewalk — minimum 4" deep at the sidewalk edge,
grading as needed (use excess soil to level pathways or add to planting areas). Spread gravel 2" deep in all areas
except plant beds, about 6" wider than your string lines so the edging in the next phase can sit on top of the
gravel. Run a vibrating plate over the gravel at least twice.

**Step 4: Entrances & cleanup.** Clean up overspill of gravel and clean up public areas.

## Phase 4: Install the edge

### Steps

**Step 1: Materials.** Bring your edge materials (see the cost estimate and assembly instructions). Remove the
erosion-protection fence; tree protection should stay unless its location interferes with edge installation.

**Step 2: Assemble.** Assemble gabion baskets (refer to your Gabion Wall assembly instructions).

**Step 3: Installation.** Set up string lines so gabion baskets sit in a straight line along the park edge. Lay out
the baskets according to your plan and fill the bottoms with 2–3" of gravel. Make small adjustments so the baskets
are level and straight relative to each other — when used alone as tables or benches, use a leveling tool on top.
Add wood edges where needed (see the Wood Edging assembly instructions).

## Phase 5: Spread topsoil

### Steps

**Step 1: Materials.** Remove the tree protection fence. Locate a stockpile area for topsoil on the sidewalk or
street — if so, apply for a temporary no-parking permit (see Phase 3).

**Step 2: Soil installation.** Prepare planting beds to receive topsoil by grading the existing soil. Spread topsoil
approximately 4–6" deep and grade it for smoothness.

**Step 3: Clean up.** Clean up overspill of topsoil and clean up public areas.

## Phase 6: Plant

### Steps

**Step 1: Plants.** Remove any weeds that have emerged. Make sure plant deliveries are scheduled and you've located a
plant delivery staging area. Lay out trees and shrubs in their desired locations, then clean up overspill of soil and
public areas.

**Step 2: Installation.** Install large trees first (see the planting detail below). Install shrubs, fine-grade the
soil, plant perennials, install 4" of mulch, then water all plants thoroughly — repeat twice a week until the plants
go dormant for winter.

## Phase 7: Install the gravel surface

### Steps

**Step 1: Materials.** Prepare for deliveries if not already stockpiled. Locate a stockpile area for gravel on the
sidewalk or street (temporary no-parking permit, if needed — see Phase 3).

**Step 2: Installation.** Install the gravel surface using a wheelbarrow and landscape rake. Install gravel
approximately 2" deep, spreading any excess evenly. Tamp down the aggregate with a vibratory plate to reduce
settling, and move through all areas to make sure they're level and even.

**Step 3: Clean up.** Clean up overspill of gravel and clean up public areas.

## Phase 8: Install park elements

### Steps

**Step 1: Measure.** Review your assembly manuals and measure and mark out where your park elements need to go.

**Step 2: Materials.** Make sure you have all the materials outlined in your assembly manuals.

**Step 3: Assemble.** Assemble your park elements according to the assembly manuals.

**Step 4: Install.** Make sure you have proper room within your design to install the elements, then locate them in
their final positions. You may be able to — or choose to — assemble elements ahead of time or off site.
