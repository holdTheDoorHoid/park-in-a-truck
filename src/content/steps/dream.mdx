Designing a Park in a Truck park is like buying a new dress or suit. First, you go to the store and check out the
mannequins to pick out a style, color or pattern that you like. Of course, what looks good hanging on the rack may not
work for you. So you must find the suit or dress in a size that fits you and your body type, then you try it on. If it
doesn't fit, you can alter it. Finally, you add accessories to truly customize it.

Just like clothes shopping, your park needs to suit your style and the intended use. Park in a Truck parks come in
four different styles — we call them **themes**: Edible, Sanctuary, Nature and Event. Each one is best suited for a
specific occasion or situation.

Now, the real fun begins — creating a vision for your park! This step starts with choosing one of the four themed
designs. However, that's just the beginning: each theme includes a set of mix-and-match **park pieces** that let you
build a **custom park** sized to your own lot.

## The four themes

Each theme emphasizes different ways you may use your park: some are well suited for neighborhood gatherings, while
others are better suited for solitude. Compare the number of trees, amount of sun and lot location you recorded on
your Assess Summary with the general requirements below.

Since every park has three parts — a **frame**, a **front** and a **back** — you can use one theme across the whole
park, or **mix and match** different themes, the same way you'd pair a top and jacket with the bottom of an outfit.

### Base design elements (every theme)

A few key elements appear in all four themes:

- **Gabion wall.** Wire baskets filled with stone give the park a distinct edge — fill them with found materials like
  brick and broken concrete for lower-cost, more sustainable construction.
- **Bench.** A place to sit, easily customized by your community. Paint them or let them weather — either works.
- **Rain barrel.** Every park needs water. Rain barrels collect stormwater for watering trees and flowers.
- **Plants.** Native plants that attract wildlife, increase biodiversity and promote conservation.

## Park pieces & how to use them

Your park design template is built from different components — **park pieces**. There are FRONT and BACK pieces for
the park's interior, and a FRAME to put them in. Each piece comes in one of the four themes, and every piece is
interchangeable, so you can mix and match between themes or choose one theme for the whole park.

Like the frame around a family photo, the **frame** creates the border around the park's interior — a welcoming
location for entrances, trees and other features. The interior is divided into the **front** (closest to the park
entrance, holding the major fixed elements) and the **back** (typically more open, for flexible use).

Images in the toolkit are shown in 3D **axon** view, like a photograph, to make them easy to picture. To actually lay
out your park you'll use a flat **plan** view that looks like a simple map — both views show the same things.

## Right size your park

If your lot perfectly fits your pieces, skip ahead to adding your existing conditions. If there's extra room, expand
your park by adding seams — feet of extra frame, front or back — so you use all the available space.

There are two kinds of seams: a **length seam**, cut between the front and back piece, adds feet along the park's
long edge; a **width seam**, trimmed along the length, adds feet along the short edge. Both seams have black dashed
lines marking every 4 feet and grey dashed lines marking every 1 foot, so you can count out exactly how much you
need.

## Add existing conditions

Transfer the existing conditions from the site plan you drew in the Assess workbook onto your design plan. Place the
two plans side by side and copy each feature over — the grids are the same, so just count the boxes to find where
things land. If an existing tree lands in a planting area, great; if it lands in gravel, that's fine too (the Create
step explains how to handle it). If your existing conditions clash with your design — a tree on top of a bench, say —
you can remove the bench by crossing it out, or shift the whole front or back piece. The design is a template: it's
somewhat rigid, but you can always add or remove things to make it work for you.

<Callout kind="auto">
  Existing trees, downspouts, wet areas, hydrants and utility lines you marked in Assess are already placed on the
  planner — drag anything that's in the wrong spot.
</Callout>
